Stocks don't trade like crypto. Lending should know that.

Tokenized stocks are onchain now, but they mostly sit idle. Generic DeFi lending treats them like tokens that trade every second, and that breaks in three predictable ways.

76%

Of the week, the market is closed

Nights and weekends add up to 128 of 168 hours. Prices freeze, but onchain loans keep running.

Prices jump at the opening bell

Overnight news lands all at once at 9:30am. A protocol that liquidates on the first print punishes borrowers and can leave bad debt.

Splits and dividends change what you hold

A 2-for-1 split doubles your balance and halves the price. Accounting that isn't built for it misprices every position.

A lending market that reads the market clock

Five rules, each enforced onchain, make tokenized equities safe to borrow against at any hour.

Two borrow limits, one for each session

While the market trades, NVIDIA backs loans up to 60% of its value. Overnight, new loans stop at 40%, because nobody knows where it will open.

A gap reserve that pays first

After-hours loans pay a 0.30% fee into a reserve. When a gap creates bad debt, the reserve covers it before lenders lose a cent.

One night, two protocols

A borrower holds 30 tNVDA and owes $2,775. Bad news breaks after the close. Scroll to see what happens next.

  1. 1

    4:00 pm. The market closes.

    tNVDA's last price is $185. AfterHours switches every stock to its overnight borrow limit and starts charging a small fee on new loans. Existing loans are untouched.

  2. 2

    11:40 pm. News breaks.

    Nobody can trade, so the price is frozen. AfterHours won't liquidate anyone on a price that can't move, so the borrower sleeps through it.

  3. 3

    9:30 am. It opens 30% lower.

    tNVDA prints at $129.50 and the health factor falls to 0.98. A generic protocol liquidates in the first block. AfterHours waits for a real opening price and starts a 15-second grace period.

  4. 4

    9:30:15 am. Grace period ends.

    The borrower had a window to repay. They didn't, so liquidation runs normally: half the loan is repaid and the liquidator receives tNVDA at a 6% discount.

  5. 5

    And if the gap is huge?

    In our test, a 60% gap left $1,000 of debt that no collateral could cover. The gap reserve paid it. Lenders lost nothing.
